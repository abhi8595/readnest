CHAPTER 12 · ARRAKIS

The Gom Jabbar

In the week before their departure to Arrakis, when all the final scurrying about had reached a nearly unbearable frenzy, an old crone in a black gown appeared before Paul in the hall.

“Have you ever heard of the Gom Jabbar?” she asked, her voice like dry leaves over stone.

Paul shook his head. The old woman smiled — if the thin stretching of her lips could be called a smile — and produced a small black box from the folds of her gown.

“Put your right hand in the box,” she said. “And remember: I must not fear. Fear is the mind-killer.”

The test of humanity, she explained, was simple only in the telling. An animal caught in a trap will gnaw off its own leg to escape. A human will remain, endure the pain, and feign death — that the trap might destroy the hunter instead.

· · ·

Outside, the wind moved through the Caladan pines, and somewhere below, the sea folded itself against the rocks in slow, patient rhythms — as if the whole planet were breathing in its sleep.

Paul placed his hand inside the box.
